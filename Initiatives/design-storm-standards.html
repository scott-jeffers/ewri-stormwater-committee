Design Storm Standards

Toward a national standard for design storms that conform to NOAA precipitation frequency estimates at every duration.

Lead: Mitch Heineman Status: Active February 2026

Overview

Design storm selection varies widely between jurisdictions, which leads to inconsistent modeling practice and results that are difficult to compare. Many agencies still rely on legacy SCS distributions that were never intended to match modern precipitation frequency estimates.

This task committee is developing guidance for a national design storm standard: how to shape hyetographs so that they conform to NOAA Atlas depths at all durations, how to select return periods and temporal distributions, and how to transition as NOAA Atlas 15 replaces Atlas 14.

Focus areas

  • Design storm shaping that is consistent with Atlas 14/15 at every duration
  • Replacing outdated SCS Type distributions in local criteria
  • Regional climate differences and nonstationarity
  • Lessons from radar-rainfall analysis of historic storm patterns

Recent updates

  1. February 2026

    Task committee formed at the February committee meeting with Mitch Heineman as lead and Constantine Karos as deputy. Members noted that NOAA expects preliminary Atlas 15 volumes for the contiguous U.S. in late 2026 to early 2027, with final release in 2027.
