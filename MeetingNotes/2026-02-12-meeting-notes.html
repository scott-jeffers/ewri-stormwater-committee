Summary

The meeting introduced new committee members Elango Thevar, Omid Razavi, Cody, Constantine Karos, and Julia Atayi, while Erica McKenzie announced a shift in committee officers to Erica McKenzie (Chair), Michael Gregory (Vice Chair), and Scott Jeffers (Secretary). The committee affirmed its identity as a "tool neutral committee" open to all storm water modeling tools, with Scott Jeffers emphasizing the need to remain open to tools like HEC-RAS and HydroCAD. The committee established three new task committees: the Design Storm National Standard Task Committee led by Mitch Heineman, the Charles Ry Legacy Committee led by Robert Dickinson, and a task committee focused on making SWMM contributions easier, which Bryant McDonnell will lead the administrative formation of. Additionally, the committee reestablished the Real-time Modeling Task Committee, led by Robert Dickinson and supported by Bryant McDonnell, and the Storm Water Modeling Teaching Tools Task Committee led by Michael Gregory and Scott Jeffers. Finally, Robert Dickinson presented "Vibe Coding" as a new technology for creating model interfaces, and Scott Jeffers provided an update on "New SWMM" development, noting an upcoming beta version with faster runtimes and a new GUI.

Details

  • Meeting Start and Participant Introductions The meeting started with informal conversation, including Mitch Heineman sharing that they were at a bowling alley Robert Dickinson sought help on screen sharing in Google Meet, with Scott Jeffers and Michael Gregory providing assistance Scott Jeffers introduced Elango Thevar as a new committee member. Elango Thevar, a water resource engineer who worked with Mitch Heineman, introduced themself and their work with AI and open-sourcing tools, particularly their interest in SWMM and EPANET Scott Jeffers highlighted Elango Thevar's achievement in using an AI chatbot to run IMP files and discuss results, suggesting they lead a "Modeler Minute" .
  • New Member Introductions SE Clark introduced their PhD student, Omid Razavi, who is working on SWMM modeling, soil compaction, and infiltration issues Omid Razavi expressed happiness to be there and noted their background in EPANET but limited experience in SWMM, seeking to gain experience and learn how to use AI for better SWMM models Cody, a project engineer with Southwest Engineering, was introduced by Scott Jeffers Cody, who is deaf and communicating through an ASL interpreter, mentioned being new to the area and focusing on HEC-RAS and StormCAD, expressing a desire to learn from others about storm water modeling, although not prioritizing work on SWMM or AI. Constantine Karos, a hydraulic modeler, also joined and expressed their interest in keeping up with the SWMM community Julia Atayi, a student at Morgan State, also joined and expressed enthusiasm for building their SWMM skills .
  • Committee Officer Update Erica McKenzie provided an update on the committee officers, explaining a shift in roles due to Dayton transitioning out. Erica McKenzie moved from Vice Chair to Chair, Michael Gregory moved from Secretary to Vice Chair, and Scott Jeffers was recruited as the new Secretary. Erica McKenzie emphasized that the officers primarily lead the organization of meetings and seek engagement from all members to determine the committee's direction and task committee support .
  • Updates on UWRC and EWR Erica McKenzie stated that the agenda item for UWRC (Urban Water Resources Research Council) and EWR (Environmental & Water Resources Institute) updates would be very short, as officers were not able to attend the UWRC .
  • Discussion on Committee Purpose and Future Erica McKenzie initiated a discussion on the purpose and future of the committee, asking for input on the range of topics and whether to align with specific products or systems. They noted the historical context of EWRI committees, including their structure and financial support tied to producing work product, which was largely affected by the pandemic Scott Jeffers suggested hearing from the new members first .
  • New Member Input on Committee Direction Elango Thevar expressed interest in the future of the EPSM development, particularly incorporating GPU processing for 2D modeling and leveraging AI to advance SWMM tools, noting the stagnation of EPSM development compared to advancements in AI and tools like HEC-RAS 2025 Robert Dickinson shared that AI tools have significantly improved recently and know SWMM and ICM well, suggesting a new world getting close to AGI Constantine Karos' interests centered around the SWMM engine, aiming to make it more "pluggable" and easier to contribute to by maximizing unit and integration testing to improve stability and reproducibility Omid Razavi is looking forward to gaining experience in SWMM and seeing how AI can improve models Cody stated their focus is on HEC-RAS and StormCAD and they are here to learn from the group .
  • Committee Stance on Tools Scott Jeffers remarked that while most members have a bias toward SWMM, the committee is the "storm water modeling committee" and should remain open to all tools, including HEC-RAS and HydroCAD Erica McKenzie summarized the consensus as a "tool neutral committee" that addresses topics based on member interest, encompassing any and all tools Mitch Heineman pointed out that SWMM is the only open-source software listed among those discussed .
  • Task Committee Engagement Reminder and Mobile Congress Michael Gregory gave a refresher on task committee commitments, urging members to only commit to things they are enthusiastic about and can achieve, emphasizing a preference for fewer, achievable committees. They also reminded the group about the upcoming hybrid in-person meeting at the Mobile Congress in two months Scott Jeffers offered to use AI to record and serve as secretary, uploading and sending out the notes Robert Dickinson offered to bring one of their Jabras to facilitate the hybrid meeting .
  • Real-time Modeling Task Committee (Sunset) Michael Gregory addressed the task committee on modeling of real-time systems in storm water, noting that Caleb, who was expected to provide an update, was delayed. Since there had been no activity, Michael Gregory suggested sun-setting the task committee unless someone wanted to take the lead Scott Jeffers offered a pitch to support members building tools by offering EWRI/ASCE co-branding and legitimacy for their side projects .
  • Resurgence of Real-time Modeling Task Committee Robert Dickinson expressed interest in taking up the real-time modeling task committee, mentioning their "vibe coded" "SWMM Anywhere" tool, which uses Open Street Maps to create SWMM models Bryant McDonnell, who had previously been involved with the committee, also volunteered to help Erica McKenzie clarified that the task committee has not officially sunsetted, requiring continued activity rather than reestablishment, and they will share the necessary form Bryant McDonnell raised a question about the expectation of promoting open-source versus proprietary tools, and Scott Jeffers responded that while there is a strong desire for open-source, they personally do not care as long as the content is pro-community and pro-education, avoiding sales pitches .
  • Storm Water Modeling Teaching Tools Task Committee Michael Gregory asked about the storm water modeling teaching tools committee, and Scott Jeffers noted that YouTube videos and a workshop in Alaska were deliverables Scott Jeffers stated that the main goal is to create marketing and training material for the new SWMM tool in preparation for an April workshop, and they have already developed MD files and a fully open and online SWMM manual website. Robert Dickinson added that they indexed and incorporated the SWMM engine into the manual Michael Gregory concluded that they will reestablish this committee and that there is a lot of material to report, also mentioning the expansion of the open SWMM site for posting EWRI branded training materials .
  • National Standard for Design Storms Task Committee Proposal Mitch Heineman proposed a new task committee for a national standard for design storms, noting client interest and the forthcoming Atlas 15 updates, expected to have a draft in early 2026. They highlighted the need for guidance on design storm shaping, conforming to the Atlas at all durations, and the prevalent issue of outdated SCS standards SE Clark provided an update on Atlas 15's schedule, stating preliminary volumes for CONUS (CONUS Volume 1 for historical analysis, Volume 2 for projections) are expected in late 2026/early 2027, with the final release in 2027 .
  • Formation of Design Storm Task Committee Mitch Heineman confirmed their willingness to lead the new Design Storm National Standard task committee Scott Jeffers suggested Elango Thevar might be interested due to their cutting-edge research on historic rainfall patterns using radar rainfall and development of an Atlas 14-like tool for India Constantine Karos also volunteered to be the deputy task manager, expressing enthusiasm for working with weather data and the Atlas. Michael Gregory confirmed the formation of the new task committee, with Mitch Heineman as the leader and Constantine Karos and Elango Thevar as members, with Scott Jeffers agreeing to coordinate communication and host the work product online .
  • Charles Ry Legacy Committee Proposal Michael Gregory asked Robert Dickinson if they still had the drive to lead a Charles Ry legacy committee, which Robert Dickinson confirmed, mentioning they have a "vibecoded" app praising Charles Ry and would aim to finish it by the Mobile Congress in April Charles Ry was identified as one of the founders of EWRI .
  • Proposal for Standard SWMM Test Suite Bryant McDonnell proposed developing a set of standard tests for any version of SWMM to pass to be "qualified" or "rubber stamped" as SWMM, given the number of forks of SWMM over the past 5 to 10 years Bryant McDonnell suggested involving Michael Triy and Constantine Karos in developing a standard set of tests that capture the dynamics of different methods inside SWMM Constantine Karos noted that the current legacy tests from 1980 are outdated and simple, and that a stable set of models that converges and covers all valuable SWMM features is needed. Scott Jeffers proposed a GitHub repo where the committee uploads IMP files to be used as a test repo .
  • Swim Benchmark Standardization and Version Control Constantine Karos and Robert Dickinson discussed the need for industry standards to evaluate models against community-created benchmarks, which requires a model to support INP, OUT, and RPT file standards. They highlighted the necessity of version control for benchmarks, linking them to specific versions of Swim to avoid incorporating bugs and referencing an issue where Bulis benchmarked against an outdated 2008 QAQC document They agreed that judicious version control is essential .
  • Task Committee Formation for Swim Contributions Erica McKenzie noted the enthusiasm for a task committee focused on making Swim contributions easier. Michael Gregory identified Bryant McDonnell's action item to complete the necessary paperwork for this committee and report back at the next meeting. Scott Jeffers offered to assist with the form and email communications to Bryant McDonnell and Constantine Karos .
  • Integration of Modeling with ASCE Resilience Themes SE Clark noted the alignment of the group's modeling discussions with ASCE's larger focus on resilience, particularly in the context of the single unified conference in Philadelphia in 2027 They suggested that the topics discussed could fit well within the conference's proposed thematic days, linking modeling to predicting and dealing with resilience, and incorporating federal data. SE Clark also mentioned that the call for abstracts for the 2027 conference is due early, on March 4th .
  • Introduction to Vibe Coding and AI Applications in Water Modeling Robert Dickinson presented an overview of "Vibe Coding" (now Vibe Engineering), a new technology where prompts and data sources are used to create model interfaces for mobile and desktop applications Robert Dickinson shared their experience using this technology, specifically mentioning the creation of a vibe code that combined various Swim manuals to allow interaction with historical Swim versions and the conversion from Swim 4 to Swim 5 in about an hour, an effort that previously took months or years Robert Dickinson cited Andre Kaparthy as a key figure in AI, having released a 204-line Python code that duplicates a GPT4 .
  • Vibe Coding Tools and Interactive Applications Robert Dickinson showcased numerous interactive tools developed using vibe coding, including a tool that duplicated Infosys RDI with genetic algorithms and an interactive hydrology and hydraulics lab based on Mikuel Miguel Pawns' website They explained that these apps, such as one comparing Swim 5, Swim 3, Swim 4, and EPA net examples, utilize a WASM engine, borrowing the engine from Pywim, to run Swim, analyze output, and allow interactive comparisons of different aspects like orifices and weirs Robert Dickinson also mentioned creating a tool to manage all their created tools, called Bob Swim .
  • Multi-Language WASM Engines and Open Source Philosophy Robert Dickinson demonstrated converting the Swim code to various languages including Rust, Python, Fortran, Julia, JavaScript, and Go, enabling the creation of WASM engines for each Robert Dickinson emphasized that all their work and code are posted on LinkedIn and GitHub and intended to be open source, allowing others to remix and create their own versions Robert Dickinson noted that AI models favor Swim 5 because of its good documentation and open-source nature, as well as the confidence inspired by OpenWim.org's 30-year legacy .
  • Interactive Application of Responsible Modeling Rules Robert Dickinson showcased an application based on Bill James' rules for responsible modeling, which integrates a Swim engine to create and run models based on the guidelines, making the help file interactive Robert Dickinson was able to run Swim from a cell phone using these tools, linking to their GitHub files Robert Dickinson also pointed out that Mitch Heineman’s website needs a security update, as AI systems dislike the "not secure" status .
  • Future Modeler Minute Speaker Michael Gregory announced that they would be seeking volunteers for the next Modeler Minute at the April 28th Congress meeting, clarifying that the presentation can extend up to 15 minutes, despite the name Bryant McDonnell suggested Michael Tryby to present on the testing philosophy they developed years ago, though Michael Tryby did not initially volunteer .
  • Update on New Swim Development and EPA Involvement Scott Jeffers provided an update on the new Swim development led by Caleb, mentioning that a beta version of "New Swim" is coming out soon for external testing and is currently undergoing internal testing. The new version is anticipated to have three times faster runtimes, a new GUI, and future true 2D modeling capability, as well as being Linux and Windows compatible, running on APIs Scott Jeffers noted that a new individual at the EPA is responsible for EPA Net and EPA Swim, who is strong on the Net side but distant from Swim, and suggested the testing committee could help the EPA by providing quality testing materials .
  • Funding and Organizational Structure for Swim Scott Jeffers confirmed that funding has been secured through a WRI innovation grant for initial startup activities, but not dedicated long-term funding for housing Swim or creating a funded staff position The current initiative aims to unify consulting and software firms under EWRI/ASCE, leveraging the non-profit status to build a sustainable legacy, similar to the international BMP database model Scott Jeffers expressed excitement and enthusiasm for capitalizing on the opportunity to consolidate community efforts, which will be discussed further at the Alabama workshop in April .
  • Concerns about New Swim Code Licensing and Openness Bryant McDonnell raised technical questions regarding the licensing and open-source nature of the new Swim code, asking if they are open to inviting community contributions or following a more centralized development model. Scott Jeffers acknowledged that these were important questions to be addressed with Caleb soon, as the code has not yet been publicly published Bryant McDonnell and Robert Dickinson emphasized that open source is critical, with Robert Dickinson noting that many vendors rely on Swim being open source .
  • Clarification on EPA's Role and Open Source Terminology Michael Tryby raised a concern regarding the use of the phrase "EPA rubber stamp," explaining that the EPA cannot legally endorse third-party products, which means their role and involvement need to be defined with different language Michael Tryby also clarified the distinction between "open source" and "source available," noting that EPA was never truly open source but rather source available Scott Jeffers confirmed that the EPA intends to give an official statement at the April meeting to clarify the future of Swim .
  • Conclusion and Next Steps Michael Gregory concluded the meeting, mentioning that the next meeting is scheduled for April 28th Scott Jeffers confirmed they would summarize the action items, post meeting notes online, send out emails to interested groups about forming task committees, and start organizing task committee activities on the website .
